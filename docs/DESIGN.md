# Design Brief

> Compass should feel like a trusted chief of staff's morning memo — a single quiet page of prose that has already done the thinking, set with the typographic authority of Stripe's annual letter and the keyboard-fast, no-chrome discipline of Linear, never the twitchy telemetry of a dashboard.

This brief is the product's design voice. Every screen should feel like it. It was
authored before implementation so the whole build stays visually consistent and
intentional — not templated.

## Principles

- It is a document, not a dashboard. The reading column is the product: one measure, six sections, top to bottom, legible in one thumb-scroll on a phone at 8:45am. No panels, no tiles, no charts, no grid of cards competing for the eye. If something can be a sentence, it is a sentence.
- Every claim carries its receipt, but receipts never interrupt the read. Numbers, PR references, SHAs and Jira keys sit inline in mono; their evidence lives one tap away in a gutter or sheet. Prose stays uninterrupted at first read and fully auditable on second.
- Colour is rationed to two meanings only. Emerald marks verified positive fact — a crossed completion threshold, a Win, a resolved blocker. Teal marks the manager's own authorship — memos, corrections, dismissals, accepted recommendations. Everything else, including risk and severity, is carried by zinc, weight, and rule thickness. Compass never colour-codes bad news.
- Honest degradation over confident polish. Low confidence, thin data, missing deploy signal and disconnected sources are typeset as first-class stated facts in the prose voice ('no deploy signal available'), never hidden, never a grey skeleton box. The calibration verdict is allowed to undercut the number directly beneath it.
- Continuity is the interface. Because nothing repeats unchanged, the design's job is to show sameness and change: a recurring item keeps its identity and visibly accrues age and history rather than reappearing as a fresh row.

## Colour & tokens

- **Palette:** `emerald-zinc`
- **Accent:** Emerald-600 is the only saturated colour in the default read, and it appears at most a handful of times per report: the crossed rung of a completion ladder, a Wins section marker, the 'resolved' state of a previously-open blocker, and the primary action in a confirm dialog. Never as a fill behind prose, never as a header bar, never on a chart. Teal-500 is strictly the manager's-hand colour: memo attribution marks, correction notes, and the state of feedback the manager has already given (dismissed, snoozed, accepted) — so scanning the page reveals at a glance which parts of reality Compass was told versus computed. Risk and blocker severity use zinc only: a 1px zinc-300 left rule at low, 2px zinc-500 at medium, 3px zinc-900 at high, in dark mode zinc-700/500/100. Emerald and teal never touch the same element. On zinc-950 backgrounds, step to emerald-400 and teal-400 for contrast; verify 4.5:1 for any coloured text.

Define the palette once as design tokens (CSS variables / Tailwind `theme.extend.colors`)
and reference the tokens everywhere — never hard-code ad-hoc colours in components.

## Typography

Two voices, one numeral system. Narration is a text serif — Source Serif 4 (fallback Newsreader, then Georgia) — at 18px/1.65 on desktop, 17px/1.6 on phone, zinc-800 (zinc-200 dark), measure capped at 66ch. Chrome, labels, buttons, nav, memo composer and all forms are Inter, 13–14px, zinc-500/600, with section headers in 11px Inter uppercase, 0.09em tracking, zinc-500, preceded by a fixed section numeral. All data tokens — percentages, point counts, dates, day counts, PR numbers, SHAs (7 chars), Jira keys, velocity figures — are Geist Mono (fallback ui-monospace) at 0.92em with font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums, zinc-900/zinc-100, slightly tighter tracking, and never bolded. Scale: report title 28px Inter semibold; section header 11px caps; body 18px serif; evidence and footnote 13px Inter zinc-500; the projected completion date is the one oversized moment at 22px mono with its confidence band at 13px directly under it. Bold inside prose is reserved exclusively for person names and entity names — nothing else is ever bolded in narration, so a name always means 'someone specific is implicated in an action here'.

## Layout

Phone-first single column: 20px side padding, content max-width 640px, sections separated by 40px of air and a single hairline (1px zinc-200 / zinc-800) that runs the full column width. From 1024px up, the page becomes a three-track reading spread on a 4px base scale (spacing steps 4/8/12/16/24/40/64): a 200px left spine rail holding the six section names as sticky in-page anchors plus the report date and team switcher; the 640px reading column, horizontally centred in the viewport, not in the remaining space; and a 320px right evidence gutter that is empty by default and fills only when a claim is expanded. Density is deliberately split: prose is airy (1.65 leading, 40px between sections), evidence is dense (13px, 1.45 leading, 8px rows, tabular alignment) — the contrast between the two is a large part of the product's signature. Merged cross-team reports use the same single column with team names as inline run-in headers, never as tabs or side-by-side columns. Everything is left-aligned and ragged-right; nothing is centred except the page itself.

## Signature elements

These are the distinctive, repeatable elements that make this product recognisable:

- The Six Spine: a persistent left rail listing the six fixed sections — 01 Yesterday, 02 Progress, 03 Blockers, 04 Risks, 05 Recommendations, 06 Wins — in the same order in every report forever, with the active section marked by a 2px emerald tick and each entry showing a mono count of items. On phone it collapses to a 44px-tall sticky strip of six numerals with the same counts. The order never reorders, so managers learn the geography of the page in two days and read it like a familiar newspaper.
- Completion ladder notches: every Yesterday item ends with a five-notch inline meter — five 3px×10px marks, hairline zinc-300 for uncrossed and solid emerald-600 for crossed — followed by a mono rung label (R2 merged, R4 released). Unreachable R5 renders as a hollow notch with the literal words 'no deploy signal available' in zinc-500 italic serif. This tiny five-notch mark, repeated down the Yesterday list, is the single most recognisable object in the product.
- Elapsed-fact sigils: recurring items carry a mono day counter set in a 1px-bordered pill immediately after the entity name ('day 6'), and a hairline horizontal trail of up to fourteen 2px ticks showing which prior days this same item ID appeared — filled ticks for days present, gaps for days absent. Change since yesterday is stated as a run-in italic serif clause ('—— reviewer added, age unchanged'), never a badge that says 'NEW'.
- Superscript evidence markers and the evidence gutter: any computed claim carries a small mono superscript marker (¹ ² ³) that reveals, in the right gutter on desktop or a bottom sheet on phone, the exact chain — Jira key → PR number → SHA → branch topology, or for alignment verdicts, the literal matched text with the matching span underlined and its confidence score in mono. Unattributed work renders as a question in serif italic ('3 commits could not be tied to a sprint objective') with the same marker, never as an accusation.
- The confidence collar: the projected completion date appears once per report inside square-bracket mono delimiters at 22px, with a two-line collar beneath it — line one the confidence band and method, line two the calibration verdict in the product's own plain voice, in zinc-600 serif italic ('your points haven't tracked elapsed days for four sprints, so this date is a cycle-time guess'). When calibration is poor the date is set in zinc-500 rather than zinc-900, so the type itself loses conviction.
- The memo line: a single-line, borderless, serif-italic prose input pinned at the bottom of the report ('Tell Compass something it doesn't know —'), with a teal-500 caret. On submit it resolves in place into the typed assertion as a teal-marked marginal note showing kind, subject, and effective dates; a refusal renders in the same voice ('I can't represent that yet'), and ambiguity offers 2–3 candidate chips. Manager memos remain visible thereafter as teal marginalia in the reports they influence.
- The date scrubber: a fixed bottom rail of one 6px tick per day of seeded history, current day marked by a taller emerald tick, arrow-key and swipe steppable, with the date in mono above it. Scrubbing regenerates through the real pipeline; items that persist across days animate in place rather than remounting, so a manager watches the Release 2 slip migrate from Risks to Recommendations as one continuous object.

## Components

- Buttons: three tiers only. Primary is emerald-600 solid, 8px radius, 36px tall, Inter 13px medium, white text — allowed at most once per view and reserved for irreversible confirmation (revoke link, create share link). Secondary is a 1px zinc-300 outline on transparent with zinc-800 text. Tertiary is bare zinc-500 text with a 1px underline appearing on hover. Feedback actions (dismiss, snooze, accept, mark wrong) are tertiary and live in a hairline-topped row at the foot of each item, revealed at full opacity on hover on desktop and always visible on touch — never hidden behind a kebab menu, since one-click feedback is a core promise.
- Cards: essentially absent. Report items are separated by 24px of space and, where grouping is needed, a single left hairline rule whose weight encodes severity. No rounded containers, no fills, no drop shadows anywhere in the reading column. Shadow is permitted only on genuinely floating layers (evidence sheet, command palette, dialogs): a single soft shadow, plus a 1px zinc-200/zinc-800 border to define the edge.
- Inputs: bottom-hairline only (1px zinc-300), no box, 40px tall, Inter 14px, label above in 11px caps zinc-500; focus raises the hairline to 2px emerald-600 with no glow and no ring offset. The memo composer is the exception and stays borderless serif italic. Errors appear as a 13px zinc-800 sentence under the field with a 2px zinc-900 left rule — never red, never an icon.
- Navigation: no top app bar with a logo band. The report date and team name are the page's own masthead, set in the reading column; team/report switching, archive and time-travel live in the left spine. Global movement is a Cmd-K command palette (jump to date, switch team, open archive, write memo) — Linear-grade keyboard coverage is expected, with j/k to move between items and d/s to dismiss/snooze the focused one.
- Loading: never a spinner and never grey skeleton blocks in the reading column. Report generation shows the six spine section labels with a 1px emerald progress hairline advancing under the active one and a mono status line in the product's voice ('reconciling 4 sprints · 12 identities'). Sections stream in as they resolve, in fixed order, so the page never reflows upward.
- Empty and degraded states: because cold start always renders a real report, the true 'empty' cases are absence-of-signal and disconnected sources. Render each as a stated sentence in serif zinc-500 in place ('no deploy signal available', 'Slack disconnected 6h ago — Blockers may be incomplete'), and surface a persistent freshness line under the masthead: 'Ingested through 08:12 · GitHub, Jira · Slack stale 6h' in 13px mono, with the stale source underlined teal-free zinc-600. A team with no sprint gets Kanban semantics, not an empty sprint widget.
- Errors: narration fallback to the deterministic template renderer is disclosed, not hidden — a hairline note above the report reading 'rendered from template — narration unavailable' in 13px zinc-500. Page-level failures use the same single-column voice with one tertiary retry action; no illustrated error art, no apologetic mascot.
- Read-only permalink view for directors: identical typography and spine, feedback affordances and the memo line removed entirely rather than disabled, plus a 13px zinc-500 line stating the report date, source freshness and share expiry. It should read like a document that was written, not an app with its controls greyed out.
- Person names: rendered as bold serif inline, and only ever adjacent to a specific object and action. Names are never sortable, never aggregated into a list of people, never adjacent to a count of commits or lines. A hover card on a name shows only role, team, current availability from memos, and open review load in mono — no productivity figures, by design and by code.

## Motion

Motion exists to preserve identity and to acknowledge input, nothing else. Standard transition is 180ms cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1); 150ms for hover and focus, 240ms for the evidence gutter and bottom sheet, which slide 8px and fade rather than scaling. Sections streaming in during generation fade up 6px, staggered 40ms in fixed order. The one expressive moment is the time-travel scrubber: as 'now' steps, items with a persisting stable ID translate to their new position over 220ms and cross-fade only their changed clause, so continuity is visually asserted; items that genuinely appear or leave fade over 160ms without movement. Feedback actions animate the item to 60% opacity with a strikethrough hairline over 180ms before it settles into its dismissed state, giving an undo window. No parallax, no looping shimmer, no animated numbers counting up, no gradient movement. All of it respects prefers-reduced-motion by collapsing to opacity-only transitions at 120ms.

## Craft references

- Stripe annual letter / Stripe Press — serif prose set with authority, data inline and typographically disciplined
- Linear — keyboard-first navigation, hairline borders, restrained single-accent chrome, zero decorative surface
- The Economist Espresso — a fixed, finite set of sections read on a phone before the day starts
- Bloomberg Terminal (typography only) — mono tabular data treated as the most trustworthy possible object on the page
- Every.to / Readwise Reader — long-form reading column, footnote-style evidence in the margin
- Vercel dashboard — honest system-status language and degradation stated as plain fact
- Basecamp Heartbeat / Hey — the memo-as-interface voice, writing to the system in one sentence
